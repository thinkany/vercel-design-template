## Part A: the brief

### A0. The first field (under 400 characters)

<!-- HELP: This is the one part that goes in the app's first field, not just in this
file. It seeds the design direction, so write it like you'd describe the site to a
designer in a lift: what it is, who it's for, how it should feel. A few sentences,
no lists. Count the characters; the field stops at 400. -->

Marlow & Finch is a small architecture studio in Bristol that designs homes and
community buildings people actually live in. The site should feel like the studio:
calm, warm, precise, with the work doing the talking. Big photographs, quiet type, no
hype. It is for private clients planning a house or an extension, and for councils
and trusts commissioning small public buildings.

### A1. What the site is

<!-- HELP: The full version of A0, as long as it needs to be. Cover: what the
organisation does, who the site is for (each audience and what they come for), the

Marlow & Finch is a six-person architecture practice founded in 2014 by Ada Marlow
and Tom Finch. The studio works on private houses, extensions and refurbishments,
and on small public buildings: libraries, community halls, a primary school. The
work is known for daylight, natural materials and a plain honesty about structure.

The site has two jobs. First, to show the work well enough that a private client
with a plot or a house feels confident enough to get in touch. Second, to give a
council officer or a trust the studio's credentials in one visit: completed public
projects, awards, the process, the people.

Audience: private clients (couples and families, mostly in the South West, budgets
from around £150k) and commissioning bodies (parish and city councils, charitable
trusts, school governors). A third, smaller audience is graduates looking to join
the studio.

Project type: website. Deliverable: a marketing site, 5 pages, one Projects
collection (the portfolio) and one Journal collection (news and short essays).

### A5. Voice

<!-- HELP: How the site should sound. Give three or four real sentences the
organisation has already written (a brochure, an email, an old site) so the agent
can match them, then a short list of rules: person (we / you), sentence length,
words to use, words to avoid. -->

The studio writes in the first person plural, plainly, in short sentences. Examples
from its own material:

- "We design buildings for the people who will live in them, not for the
  photographs. The photographs tend to follow."
- "Every project starts with a walk around the site, whatever the weather."
- "We'd rather tell you a plan won't work in the first meeting than in the
  eighteenth."

Tone: warm, direct, unhurried. Say what the studio does, not how special it is. No
exclamation marks. Avoid "bespoke", "passion", "journey", "dream home" and
"stunning". Use "house" not "residence", "client" not "customer".

### A6. Assets

<!-- HELP: What you already have and where it is: logo files, photography, video,

- Team portraits: six, shot together in the studio, consistent light (in the

- Video: none. A short site walk-through may come later.
- No stock photography anywhere. Every image is a real project or a real person.

<!-- HELP: Two or three sites whose structure or feel is worth learning from, each

- Direction: quiet and generous. Photographs large, type small and confident, lots

### A8. Watch-outs

<!-- HELP: The things that would be wrong if the agent didn't know them: legal
lines, claims not to make, features to design but leave hidden, accessibility
requirements, anything the client has been burned by before. -->

- **Chartered practice**: the footer carries "RIBA Chartered Practice" and the
  company number. Don't shorten or restyle the RIBA line.
- **Client privacy**: private houses are named by area only ("A house in
  Clifton"), never by street or client name. Public projects use their real names.
- **Testimonials**: three quotes are real and cleared (§B1). Never invent one and
  never add a photo of a client.
- **Accessibility**: the studio does public work and the site will be checked by
  councils. AA contrast everywhere, real alt text on every project photo, the form
  usable by keyboard.
- **Claims**: no awards beyond those listed in §B1. No "award-winning" as a phrase.

### B3. Studio

**Hero**
- Heading: The studio
- Body: Founded in 2014 by Ada Marlow and Tom Finch. Six people, one floor of a

**Prose: how we work**
- Heading: How we work
- Body: Every project starts with a walk around the site, whatever the weather.
  We draw by hand before we draw on screen, and we make models you can hold. We'd
  rather tell you a plan won't work in the first meeting than in the eighteenth.
  Budgets are discussed early and often, in plain numbers.

**Steps: a project, stage by stage**
- Heading: What happens, in order
- 1. A first conversation. Free, an hour, at the site if we can. We say whether we
  think we're right for the job.
- 2. Brief and feasibility. We measure, survey and test ideas against the budget
  and the planning context. You get a short written report.
- 3. Design. Concept, then developed design, then planning. Models, drawings,
  choices made together.
- 4. Technical design and tender. The building fully drawn and specified, priced
  by builders we trust.
- 5. On site. We visit weekly, answer the builder's questions, and see it through
  to the last snag.

**Team**
- Heading: Who you'll work with
- Ada Marlow, Founding Director. Houses, materials, the drawing board.
- Tom Finch, Founding Director. Public buildings, planning, the difficult
  conversations.
- Priya Nair, Associate. Runs projects on site; the person the builder rings.
- Ben Okonjo, Architect. Refurbishments, timber, the workshop.
- Hannah Lowe, Part II Architectural Assistant. Models and visualisation.
- Marcus Reid, Practice Manager. Fees, contracts, and the kettle.

**FAQ**
- Heading: Questions people ask
- Do you take on small projects? Yes. An extension or a loft is a proper project
  and gets the same director-led attention as a new house.
- What does an architect cost? Our fees are a percentage of the build cost,
  typically between 10 and 14 percent depending on the stage we're appointed to
  and the complexity. We set this out in writing before any work starts.
- Do you work outside Bristol? Across the South West and South Wales, and further
  for the right project.
- Will you get planning permission? We can't promise it; nobody honest can. We can
  say we know the local authorities well and our record is good.
- Can you recommend a builder? We tender each project to two or three contractors
  we've worked with before and trust.
- Do you take on graduates? We usually have one Part II place. Write to Marcus with
  a short portfolio.

- Title: Fishponds Library opens its doors
- Tags: Studio news
- Body:
  After three years, two consultations and one very wet winter on site, Fishponds
  Library opened on Saturday. Around four hundred people came through in the first
  afternoon, which is roughly four hundred more than the old building saw in a
  week.

  The building is simple: one long room under a timber roof, lit from the north,
